# Quality System and SOP Register

The Category D licence is issued on the applicant's **attestation** that minimum standards
are already met, and the licence holder is inspected during the licence term to verify that
attestation. This register sets out the procedures that must exist, what each must contain,
and the records each must generate.

**Rule for this engagement:** nothing is attested that has not been written, approved and
made effective. Where an activity has not yet commenced, an approved SOP plus blank record
templates is an acceptable position — overstating readiness is not.

---

## 1. Mandatory procedures

These four are explicitly identified in SAHPRA's Category D guidance. `[VERIFY]`

| SOP | Must cover | Records generated |
|---|---|---|
| **Quality assurance product release** | Who is authorised to release imported stock for sale; the checks performed before release (consignment documents, certificate of analysis against specification, label check, quantity, batch number, expiry, transport conditions); quarantine until released; what happens on a failed check | Release register; signed release record per consignment/batch |
| **Recall** | Classification of recall levels; decision authority; how far down the supply chain stock is traced; customer notification templates; SAHPRA notification; retrieval, segregation and reconciliation of recalled stock; effectiveness check; closure report; periodic mock recall | Recall file; distribution records enabling traceability; mock recall report |
| **Finished product specifications and testing** | How the specification for each product is established and approved; incoming verification against specification; reliance on the manufacturer's certificate of analysis and when independent testing is triggered; out-of-specification handling | Approved specification per product; certificates of analysis on file; OOS investigations |
| **Determination of shelf life (expiry date)** | Basis for the shelf life claimed; the stability data relied on; how expiry dating is verified on receipt; minimum remaining shelf life accepted at import; handling of short-dated and expired stock | Shelf-life justification per product; stock expiry register |

## 2. Supporting procedures expected at inspection

A licence holder operating only the four procedures above will struggle at inspection.
Build these alongside.

| SOP | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Receipt and goods-in | Physical receipt, damage inspection, temperature check on arrival, quarantine placement |
| Storage and stock control | Segregation, FEFO rotation, temperature monitoring and excursion handling, stock reconciliation |
| Distribution and dispatch | Picking, packing, transport conditions, delivery documentation, traceability to customer |
| Returns | Acceptance criteria, assessment, decision to return to saleable stock or destroy |
| Rejected and expired stock | Segregation, security, authorised destruction and destruction records |
| Complaints | Receipt, logging, investigation, response, trending, escalation to the manufacturer |
| Adverse event reporting | How a report is received, recorded and reported onward; timelines |
| Self-inspection / internal audit | Scheduled internal audit against the licence conditions, with CAPA |
| Deviations and CAPA | Recording deviations, root cause, corrective and preventive action, closure |
| Change control | Changes to products, suppliers, premises, responsible person — including which changes require a licence amendment |
| Document control | Versioning, approval, effective dates, withdrawal of superseded copies |
| Training | Induction and ongoing training, competency assessment, records per employee |
| Supplier and customer qualification | Verifying that suppliers and trade customers are appropriately licensed |
| Temperature monitoring and calibration | Monitoring, alarm response, calibration schedule |
| Handling of regulatory inspections | Who hosts, what is shown, how findings are managed |

## 3. Standard SOP structure

Every procedure should carry: document number and version · title · effective date · author,
reviewer and approver with signatures · review period · purpose · scope · definitions ·
responsibilities · procedure · records generated · references · revision history.

## 5. The twelve-month records rule

Once an activity has commenced, completed records covering at least the preceding twelve
months must be maintained. `[VERIFY]` The practical consequence is that record-keeping must
start on the day the first consignment is received — an inspection two years after licensing
will look for a continuous record, and a gap cannot be reconstructed afterwards.
