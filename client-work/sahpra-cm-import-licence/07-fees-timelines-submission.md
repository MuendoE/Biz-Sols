# Fees, Submission and Post-Licence Obligations

> **All figures below are indicative and unverified.** They come from a secondary summary of
> SAHPRA's gazetted fee schedule, not from the gazette or SAHPRA's fees page. Confirm every
> amount and reference format against SAHPRA's current fee schedule and fee categorisation
> guidance **before** any payment is made. An incorrectly referenced payment is a common
> cause of an application sitting unprocessed.

---

**Gaps to close:**

1. No **importer** line appears in the summary above, and no Category D-specific (DL02/DL03)
   fee. Establish which fee category an importer of complementary medicines falls into.
2. Category D licensing is reported to attract both a **licence application fee and a desktop
   evaluation fee**. Confirm whether these are separate charges and what each is.
3. Confirm whether DL02 and DL03, if both are required, attract two fees or one.

**Budget guidance for the client:** present application fee, issuing fee and the first annual
retention as a single first-year cost, and flag the recurring June retention and the
five-yearly renewal. Flag the amendment fee as the reason to get the product schedule
complete at first submission.

## 2. Payment

- Payment by electronic funds transfer to SAHPRA's bank account. Obtain current banking
  details from SAHPRA directly — never from a third-party website or an emailed instruction.
- Use the payment reference format SAHPRA prescribes in its fee categorisation guidance.
- Attach proof of payment to the application as Annexure Q.
- Keep the remittance advice and bank confirmation on the client file.

## 4. Timelines

**No verified SAHPRA turnaround time for a Category D licence application was available when
this pack was drafted, and none should be quoted to the client until confirmed.**

Plan on this sequence instead, and give the client a range only once items 1 to 3 are done:

| Phase | Driver of duration |
|---|---|
| Client data collection | Client responsiveness; product data from overseas manufacturers is usually the slowest element |
| Responsible person appointment | Recruitment and, where applicable, SAPC registration — frequently the longest lead item |
| Premises readiness | Any physical work needed: quarantine area, security, temperature monitoring |
| SOP development and approval | Cannot complete before the responsible person is appointed |
| Application compilation | Short, once the above are done |
| SAHPRA screening, queries and evaluation | Outside our control; query rounds extend it |
| Licence issue | Issuing fee payable |
| Post-licence inspection | Within the five-year licence term |

Advise the client not to commit to supply dates or launch dates until the licence is in hand.

## 5. At the port of entry

Imported health products are released at designated ports of entry. Seven are reported:
OR Tambo International Airport, King Shaka International Airport, Durban Harbour, Cape Town
International Airport, Cape Town Harbour, Gqeberha Airport and Gqeberha Harbour. `[VERIFY
against SAHPGL-INSP-RC-11 v2, 8 June 2022, and confirm the current version.]`

Consignment documentation reported as required includes the invoice, the quantities to be
released of each product, images showing the full labelling of the products, and a copy of
the importer's section 22C(1)(b) licence.

**Practical points:**

- A person may only import a medicine if licensed to do so. The licence must be in place
  **before** stock is shipped, not before it is cleared.
- Product names on the licence schedule, the invoice and the labels must match exactly.
- Establish early whether a per-consignment import permit (historically an MBR 20) applies to
  Category D consignments or only to scheduled substances. `[VERIFY]`
- Confirm whether Marine Collagen, as an animal-derived product, requires a veterinary import
  permit from DALRRD under the Animal Diseases Act 35 of 1984, and whether Port Health has any
  requirement for any line classified as a foodstuff.
- Brief the client's freight forwarder and customs broker on the SAHPRA requirements; most
  detentions arise from a broker who does not know the consignment needs SAHPRA release.

## 6. Post-licence obligations

| Obligation | Frequency | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Annual retention fee | Each June | Client, diarised by Alpha CR |
| Licence renewal | Before expiry of the five-year term | Alpha CR |
| Amendment application for new products, new premises, change of responsible person | As it arises | Alpha CR |
| Maintain SOPs and records supporting the attestation | Continuous | Client / responsible person |
| Inspection readiness | Inspection expected within the licence term | Client / Alpha CR |
| Complaints, recalls and adverse event reporting | As it arises | Responsible person |
| Monitor for a new call-up notice requiring registration of complementary medicines | Continuous | Alpha CR |
| Keep GMP certificates current for every manufacturing site | Diarise each expiry | Alpha CR |

Set these up as diarised obligations at the point the licence is issued. The commonest
failure in the year after licensing is a missed retention payment or a lapsed GMP certificate
that nobody was watching.
